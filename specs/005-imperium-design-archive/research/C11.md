Roman housing survey (research; real-history concept, no detail added)

- The survey of Roman urban houses uses two "peak periods" (盛期): peak one is ancient Rome (1st century BC to 2nd century), peak two is Eastern Rome (6th century; 10th to 12th century). `Imperium-Knowledge/designs/roman-cards/罗马住宅调查-2026-10-28.md` L2-L10, L52-L53 [HISTORY]
- The survey's convention is 1 cell is about 1 metre; areas converted to outline cells are marked "推" (inferred); "不详" means the consulted source gives none. `Imperium-Knowledge/designs/roman-cards/罗马住宅调查-2026-10-28.md` L4
- The house types listed for peak one are A1 atrium-peristyle house (中庭—列柱院宅, domus), A2 shop with loft (铺面带阁楼, taberna/pergula), A3 multi-storey rental block (多层出租楼, insula), A4 hall-type flat (中厅式套房, medianum type, Ostia), A5 provincial peristyle house without atrium (行省列柱院宅). `Imperium-Knowledge/designs/roman-cards/罗马住宅调查-2026-10-28.md` L8-L50 [HISTORY]
- The house types listed for peak two are B1 apse-hall peristyle grand house (后殿厅列柱院贵宅, 4th-6th c.), B2 colonnaded-street shop house (柱廊街铺屋), B3 multi-storey courtyard block (多层合院住楼), B4 divided old peristyle house (分割的旧列柱院宅, 6th-7th c.), B5 North Syrian stone tower house (北叙利亚石砌楼宅, 4th-6th c.), B6 middle-period small courtyard house (中期小院宅, 10th-12th c.), B7 capital aristocratic mansion (都城贵族宅邸, 11th-12th c.). `Imperium-Knowledge/designs/roman-cards/罗马住宅调查-2026-10-28.md` L54-L98 [HISTORY]
- The survey draws these continuities between the two periods: the atrium vanishes and the courtyard becomes the sole core; the apse hall replaces the main hall (tablinum); shop houses persist; floor rules change from height limits to spacing, balconies and sea view; later houses are more inward-looking; large houses get divided; storage moves into the house. `Imperium-Knowledge/designs/roman-cards/罗马住宅调查-2026-10-28.md` L102-L110 [HISTORY]
- The survey's summary table gives, per type, a suggested number of cards: A1 3 (small, medium, large slices), A2 2 (single unit, row), A3 2, A4 2 (single flat, back-to-back pair), A5 2 (flat ground, terrace slope), B1 2, B2 2, B3 1-2, B4 1-2, B5 2 (simple, complex), B6 3 (courtyard house, strip house, large house), B7 1 (partial slice); outline-cell ranges are given per type, with the notes that big houses exceed the limit (A1) and B1/B7 must be cut. `Imperium-Knowledge/designs/roman-cards/罗马住宅调查-2026-10-28.md` L114-L129
- The survey leaves one point to be verified: the 4th-century Rome list figures (about 1790 domus, insula about 25 times as many) cover Rome alone, whether an insula is one building or one rental unit is disputed, and the c. 425 Constantinople list cannot be taken as a general rule for a large Roman city. `Imperium-Knowledge/designs/roman-cards/罗马住宅调查-2026-10-28.md` L18, L34 [R-1103-1338①, 待查证; HISTORY]

City-shape principles restated in the coverage table (second-hand, from the city cards)

- On walls: the wall follows visible things (river bank, rock edge, soft-ground edge, extent by region), not drawn cell by cell; the great turns lie only at the meeting of two defence fronts and inside a front only small-angle turns; the wall first moves as whole sections, then turns as a whole line, finally splits in sections, and its outline is not a fitted symmetric shape; frequent towers mean the wall runs wrongly, and the wall's direction is not changed to seat a tower. `Imperium-Knowledge/designs/tools/city/check-覆盖表.md` L410-L413
- On density: the closer to the core and main street the narrower and denser the plots; further out plots grow larger with courtyards and gardens, and the outermost ring is mostly garden land; the city grows from its core and tends to be round, the distance from core to edge about equal in every direction, deviation only because of landforms or borrowed terrain; each city district has a small centre (small square with fountain and market), harbour areas and the inner side of gates each form small cores. `Imperium-Knowledge/designs/tools/city/check-覆盖表.md` L414-L416
- On large pieces: public buildings and colonnaded shops ring the forum with houses stepping back to a second ring; the colonnaded forum is about 3:2 in length to width; monuments sit at the edge with the centre left empty; the forum's entrances face the main building; main street type A or B is fixed by the angle of the approach roads; no two axes of equal rank; the secondary axis ends at a palace or sanctuary; the main street follows the water; the main bridge faces the first forum; the palace district lies beside the oldest core, on the steadiest side and upstream when there is a river, with its own exit. `Imperium-Knowledge/designs/tools/city/check-覆盖表.md` L417-L419
- On conspicuous plots: rock heads are not lived on, used for palace gardens and church bases; a church on a rock head stands at the end of sight line or at a turn of the main road; cypress trees in rows mark the axis and the palace district; the later expansion ring (from phase two) holds gardens, open cisterns and warehouses, and a pond taken into the city becomes a cistern. `Imperium-Knowledge/designs/tools/city/check-覆盖表.md` L420-L422
