### cities

- Roman references are limited to the Empire's core lands, Italy, Greece, Asia Minor, Syria, Egypt and North Africa, in both peaks; border-province cities and forts (London and Cirencester are named) are not references. `Imperium-Knowledge/14_来源同步与权威.md` L56 [用户 10-29 07:19] HISTORY; REPLACED-BY R-1030-1246 (参照去意大利) and R-1030-1252 (参照范围改为罗马基本盘、代表性名单去罗马城), named at L56, not in this chunk
- A feature seen in Italy or in textbooks is not taken as an imperial rule. The black basalt streets of Rome and Pompeii and the square military camp are Italian or textbook special cases; street colour, wall colour and city shape were the same mistake. First ask whether something is local to Italy or belongs to the whole Empire. `Imperium-Knowledge/14_来源同步与权威.md` L56 [用户 10-29 纠正] HISTORY
- Roundness is not a goal; the circular-polygon city of the "tower spacing comparison" picture is not a shape reference. `Imperium-Knowledge/14_来源同步与权威.md` L56 [用户 10-29 07:10] HISTORY; REPLACED-BY R-1030-1503 (趋圆, named at L56, not in this chunk)
- Representativeness test: "has existed" is not "can represent". Only what the great cities of both peaks share (Rome, Constantinople, Antioch, Alexandria, Ephesus, Thessalonica and the like) enters a rule. A plan seen in small towns, camps or veteran colonies (the square city of Timgad, the near-round polygon of Nicaea) only exists and is no rule. Ancestors for lineage checks are sought among great cities, not by count. The aim is what can represent Roman civilisation. `Imperium-Knowledge/14_来源同步与权威.md` L56 [用户 10-29 07:21] HISTORY
- Nicaea measured is an irregular pentagon of about 5 km, not near-round.
